Reinforcement learning you can watch

Two small agents learn right here in your browser, followed by videos of RL agents playing real video games. The lesson covers the theory; this page shows it happening.

1Q-learning fills in a maze

The agent starts knowing nothing. Each step updates one estimate, \(Q(s,a)\), and good values spread backward from the goal one visit at a time.
Live Q-learning agent

Steps to finish each episode over the last 400 episodes (lower is better; faint line is raw, bold is a 20-episode average)

Goal (G) pays +1, pits (P) pay −1, and each step costs 0.01, so shorter paths win. The update is \(Q(s,a) \leftarrow Q(s,a) + \alpha\,[r + \gamma \max_{a'} Q(s',a') - Q(s,a)]\) with \(\alpha = 0.5\), \(\gamma = 0.95\). Set ε to 0 after it learns and watch it walk the shortest path; set it to 0 from the start and it can get stuck circling.

2A policy gradient learns to balance a pole

No value table this time: a five-number linear policy pushes the cart left or right, and REINFORCE nudges those numbers toward actions that came before long runs.
CartPole, trained by REINFORCE
trained episodes 0 · watching run 0 steps
Training episode length over the last 400 episodes (500 is the cap, meaning it never fell)

Training plays episodes fast in the background with a stochastic policy, \(\pi(\text{right}\mid s) = \sigma(w^\top s)\). The animation shows the current policy acting greedily, restarting whenever the pole tips past 12° or the cart leaves the track. It usually reaches 500 steps within a few hundred episodes, and sometimes forgets for a while afterward, which is the instability PPO was built to tame.

3RL agents in video games

Atari · DQN · 2015

DQN learns Breakout from pixels

DeepMind's Deep Q-Network sees only the screen and the score. After enough play it discovers tunneling up the side so the ball bounces behind the wall.

Reward hacking · 2016

The boat that never finishes the race

OpenAI rewarded points instead of finishing. The agent found a lagoon where targets respawn and circles forever, on fire, outscoring humans without ever completing a lap.

Multi-agent · PPO · 2019

Hide and seek invents tool use

Two teams trained against each other with only a win or lose signal. Strategies escalate: forts, ramps, ramp locking, then box surfing that exploits the physics.

Dota 2 · PPO at scale · 2018

OpenAI Five

Five networks trained with PPO on about 180 years of self-play per day. In 2019 they beat the reigning world champions, OG.

Pokémon Red · PPO · 2023

Teaching an agent to explore Kanto

Peter Whidden shows how much reward design matters when the game gives no score: novelty bonuses, level-ups, and all the ways the agent games each one.

Trackmania · 2022

An unbeatable Trackmania driver

Yosh trains a racing agent from scratch. It's a good look at how observations, rewards and training time shape what the car learns.
